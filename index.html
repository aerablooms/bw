CHAPTER ONE
Case study

Fiji's grid 2000 – 2023

Of every island group in the SPC record Fiji's demand climbs the most predictably — a near-perfect line from a diesel-lit coast to a modern tourist-driven grid.

CHAPTER THREE
Per capita

Rising demand isn't rising blame

Measured per person the story flips. Two territories sit far above the rest — for very different reasons — while most of the region emits only a fraction of the global average.

3.5 tonnes

13.2 tonnes rising

Palau — the outlier

132 tonnes on average

CHAPTER FIVE
Pattern check

Growth doesn't predict guilt

A weak statistically insignificant correlation — and a clustering model that splits the region into three very different emissions stories.

r = 0.18

Across 17 island groups a country's power-generation growth rate barely predicts its greenhouse-gas-per-capita growth rate (p = 0.53) — country-specific factors dominate instead.

Cluster 0 — 15 countries

Low & stable

From American Samoa to Vanuatu fifteen of seventeen island groups sit in one tight low-emitting cluster — the regional norm not the exception.

Cluster 1 — Palau

Extreme & falling

Palau stands alone: an emissions level an order of magnitude above its neighbours driven by fuel re-exports — and now declining sharply from that peak.

Cluster 2 — New Caledonia

Elevated & rising

The only island group that is both structurally high and still climbing — the reverse of Palau's trajectory and worth watching closest.

Closing

The tide doesn't check who caused it

Pacific grids are modernising fast mostly on imported fuel while two small territories — for opposite reasons — carry an outsized share of the region's per-person emissions story.

  • Regional power generation grew strongly and near-linearly over 2000–2023 led by New Caledonia Papua New Guinea and Fiji.
  • The region-wide decline in average per-capita emissions is dominated by one outlier — Palau falling from a very high base — not a broad-based decarbonisation.
  • Clustering confirms Palau and New Caledonia are structurally distinct from the other 15 PICTs and should be reported separately to avoid a skewed regional narrative.
  • Both five-year forecasts are naive linear extrapolations — directional estimates only not substitutes for engineering or IPCC-aligned scenario modelling.
